How your engine works, and why diagnosis comes first

An engine needs fuel, air, a spark, compression, cooling, oil and computer control all working together. A problem in one area can look like trouble in another. A rough idle, for example, can come from the ignition, fuel delivery or a sensor rather than worn parts inside the engine.

The check engine light means the car's computer detected a fault. It does not name the failed part. A code is where diagnosis starts, not a parts order. Tell us when the light came on, what the car was doing, and whether the problem changes when the engine is cold, warm or working hard.

Signs your engine needs attention

  • The check engine light stays on or flashes.
  • The engine shakes, hesitates, misfires or idles rough.
  • It's hard to start, or it stalls.
  • Acceleration feels weaker, or fuel use changes for no clear reason.
  • A new ticking, knocking or tapping noise.
  • The temperature gauge climbs, or an oil pressure warning comes on.
  • Smoke from the tailpipe that wasn't there before.

None of these signs proves the engine needs a major repair, and most have more than one possible cause. If the check engine light is flashing, or an oil pressure or overheating warning comes on, pull over somewhere safe and shut the engine off. Driving on can make the damage worse.

Cylinder head and block repair

The cylinder head sits on top of the engine block, sealed by the head gasket. A failed head gasket can let coolant into the oil or the cylinders, or push combustion gases into the cooling system. Common signs are overheating, coolant loss with no visible leak, white exhaust smoke with a sweet smell, and milky oil. Overheating can also warp a cylinder head. We test before we take the engine apart, because some of these signs have other causes.

Rebuilding an engine

A rebuild keeps your engine and replaces the worn or damaged parts inside it, such as bearings, piston rings, gaskets and seals, plus any machine work the parts need. It can make sense when the block and head are sound and the rest of the car is worth keeping. We tell you what we found inside the engine before you decide.

Replacing an engine

Sometimes a rebuild costs more than it's worth, or the damage is too severe, such as a cracked block or a seized engine. Then installing another engine can be the better option. The right choice depends on the engine's condition, the vehicle and the parts available, so we go over the options and costs with you before any work starts.

Maintenance that protects your engine

Your owner's manual lists the oil, filter, spark plug, belt and coolant intervals for your engine. Following them costs far less than most engine repairs. How you drive matters too: many manuals treat short trips, pulling a trailer and stop-and-go traffic as severe service, with shorter intervals. Bring your service records if you have them. Frequently asked questions

Does a check engine light mean my engine is failing?
No. It means the car's computer detected a fault, which can involve the ignition, fuel delivery, sensors, emissions parts or the engine itself. Diagnosis finds which one.
What causes an engine to misfire?
Worn spark plugs, a failing ignition coil, fuel delivery problems, air leaks and mechanical wear can all cause misfires. The pattern and the test results narrow it down.
Can I keep driving with a flashing check engine light?
It's best not to. A flashing light usually means a misfire bad enough to damage the catalytic converter. Pull over somewhere safe, follow your owner's manual, and call us at 610-626-4700 to talk about getting the car here.
Should I rebuild or replace my engine?
It depends on the damage, the condition of the block and head, the vehicle and the parts available. We inspect first, then go over both options and their costs before you decide.
How do I know if my head gasket is blown?
Overheating, coolant loss with no visible leak, white sweet-smelling exhaust smoke and milky oil are common signs. Other problems cause some of the same signs, so we test before taking the engine apart.
Do you work on imported cars?
Yes. We repair domestic and imported vehicles. Call with the year, make and model and we'll tell you what we can do.
