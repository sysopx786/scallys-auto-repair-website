What an alignment adjusts

An alignment sets the angles of the wheels to the manufacturer's specifications. Toe is whether the front of the tires point in or out, seen from above. Camber is the inward or outward tilt of the tire, seen from the front. Caster is the angle of the steering axis, seen from the side, which helps the wheel return to center. Small changes in these angles can make the car pull and wear tires quickly.

Signs you need an alignment

  • The car pulls or drifts to one side on a straight, level road.
  • The steering wheel is off center when you drive straight.
  • The front tires wear faster on the inside or outside edge.
  • The tires squeal in normal turns.
  • You hit a big pothole or a curb, and the car feels different since.

A pull isn't always alignment. Uneven tire pressure, a tire with an internal defect, a sticking brake caliper or a worn suspension part can make a car pull too. We check tire pressure and condition and look for loose parts before adjusting anything.

How we do it

  • Check tire pressure, tire wear and the steering and suspension parts for play.
  • Measure the wheel angles and compare them with the manufacturer's specifications.
  • Adjust the angles that can be adjusted on your car and center the steering wheel.
  • Tell you about any worn part we find before replacing it.

If a tie rod, ball joint or control arm is worn, it gets replaced first; aligning around a loose part wastes your money because the angles won't hold. See steering repair and suspension repair.

When to get an alignment

  • After replacing tie rods, struts, control arms or other steering and suspension parts.
  • When you put on new tires, to protect them from uneven wear.
  • After a hard hit to a pothole or curb.
  • Whenever you notice a pull, an off-center wheel or uneven tire wear.

Alignment vs. balancing

These two get mixed up. An alignment fixes pulling and uneven wear by adjusting wheel angles. Wheel balancing fixes vibration at speed by evening out the weight of each tire and wheel. A shaking steering wheel usually points to balance, not alignment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I need an alignment?
Common signs are the car pulling to one side, a steering wheel that's off center when driving straight, and front tires wearing on one edge.
How often should I get an alignment?
There's no single interval for every car. Check your owner's manual, and get one after new tires, steering or suspension work, a hard pothole hit, or when you notice a pull.
Can a pothole knock my car out of alignment?
Yes. A hard hit can change the wheel angles or bend a part. If the car feels different afterward, have it checked.
Will an alignment stop my steering wheel from shaking?
Usually not. Shaking at speed is more often wheel balance, a bent wheel or a worn part. An alignment fixes pulling and uneven wear.
Do I need an alignment after new tires?
It's a good idea. If the old tires wore unevenly, the same alignment problem will wear out the new ones too.
Why does my car still pull after an alignment?
A tire defect, uneven tire pressure, a sticking brake or a worn part can cause a pull that an alignment won't fix. We check those when a pull doesn't go away.
