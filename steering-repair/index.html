How your steering works

When you turn the wheel, the steering gear or rack moves the tie rods, and the tie rods turn the wheels. Ball joints let the wheels pivot and move up and down at the same time. Control arms hold each wheel in place. Power steering, either hydraulic or electric, takes most of the effort out of turning. A worn part anywhere in that chain changes how the car feels.

Signs you need steering repair

  • The steering wheel is hard to turn, especially when parking.
  • A whine or groan when you turn the wheel.
  • Red or amber fluid under the front of the car.
  • Play or looseness in the steering wheel.
  • A clunk or knock when turning or going over bumps.
  • The car wanders and needs constant corrections.
  • Uneven wear on the inside or outside edge of the front tires.

These signs overlap, and a tire or wheel problem can feel like a steering problem. We inspect for play at each joint, check the power steering system and road-test the car. If the steering suddenly gets very heavy or the car won't hold its direction, it isn't safe to drive. Stop somewhere safe and call us.

Power steering repair

Hydraulic power steering uses a pump, hoses and fluid; a leak or a failing pump often shows up as a whine, heavy steering and a low fluid level. Electric power steering has no fluid and uses a motor and sensors, so a fault there usually turns on a warning light. Tell us whether the problem shows up while parking, at low speed or on the road, and mention any warning lights.

Tie rods

Tie rods connect the steering rack to the wheels. As their joints wear, the steering gets loose, the car wanders and the front tires wear on their edges. Replacing a tie rod changes the wheel's toe angle, so an alignment usually follows.

Ball joints

Ball joints are the pivots between the control arms and the steering knuckles. A worn ball joint can clunk over bumps, let the wheel wobble and wear tires unevenly. A badly worn one can separate, which can cause loss of control, so we check them with the weight off the wheel and measure the play.

Control arms

Control arms hold the wheel in position as the suspension moves. Their rubber bushings crack and tear with age, which causes clunks, wandering under braking and uneven tire wear. Depending on the car, we replace the bushings or the whole arm, which on many cars comes with a new ball joint built in.

Steering and your Pennsylvania inspection

Steering and suspension parts are part of Pennsylvania's safety inspection, and excess play in tie rods or ball joints can keep a car from passing. As an official inspection station, we can repair what fails and finish the inspection here. See PA safety inspections.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my steering suddenly hard to turn?
Low power steering fluid, a failing pump, a slipping belt or an electric steering fault are common causes. If you can't steer normally, stop somewhere safe and call us instead of forcing it.
Why does my steering whine when I turn?
On hydraulic systems, a whine often means low fluid, usually from a leak, or a worn pump. Topping off fluid without fixing the leak only buys time.
Can worn tie rods cause loose steering?
Yes. Worn tie rod ends are a common cause of play in the wheel and wandering. Other parts can cause similar symptoms, so we check each joint.
What does a bad ball joint sound like?
Often a clunk or knock over bumps or when turning. Sound alone can't confirm it, so we check the joint for play with the weight off the wheel.
Will an alignment fix loose steering?
No. An alignment sets wheel angles; it can't fix a worn part. Worn parts get replaced first, then the car is aligned if needed.
Is it safe to drive with a bad ball joint?
A badly worn ball joint can separate and cause loss of control. If you hear clunking from the front end or feel the wheel wobble, have it checked soon, and stop driving if the steering feels unstable.
