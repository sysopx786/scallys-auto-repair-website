How automotive window tint works

Automotive window tint normally involves a film attached to an existing glass surface. Different films may be made for appearance, glare control or filtering certain kinds of sunlight, but their characteristics vary. The shop's current film brands, materials, shades and performance claims have not been confirmed. Ask which products are actually available for your car rather than assuming a particular ceramic, metallic or other film is offered.

When you see a window tint percentage, it commonly refers to visible light transmission: the amount of visible light that passes through the glass and film. A lower number represents less light passing through. The existing glass may already be tinted, so the final combined result matters. This is why buying a film labeled with a percentage does not by itself establish that the completed window meets Pennsylvania requirements.

Questions to consider before tinting

  • Is the vehicle a passenger car, a truck or a qualifying multipurpose vehicle?
  • Which windows do you want to tint?
  • Does the glass already have a factory tint or aftermarket film?
  • Is forward and side visibility comfortable in poor weather or at night?
  • Is the desired appearance consistent with Pennsylvania's light-transmission requirements?
  • Are there any special glass coatings or features that require compatibility checks?
  • Will the owner need information about a documented exemption?

What Pennsylvania law says about window tint

PennDOT's April 2025 Vehicle Window Tint fact sheet explains the standard visible-light-transmission limits. For passenger cars manufactured in 1998 or later, the windshield, front side, rear side and rear windows generally must each allow at least 70% of visible light through. For older passenger cars, the rear window has a separate VESC-20 rule. Requirements differ for trucks and multipurpose passenger vehicles, so do not apply one passenger-car chart to every vehicle.

For trucks and vehicles that qualify as multipurpose passenger vehicles under PennDOT's definitions, the windshield and front side windows generally still have a 70% minimum, while the rear side and rear window categories in the fact sheet have no listed minimum. The exact classification and any applicable exception matter. The legal standard concerns the total light passing through the window, not the seller's film label alone.

Safety inspections and citations are different

PennDOT states that Pennsylvania removed the routine tint check from the vehicle safety inspection process in 1996. That does not make noncompliant tint legal. The agency says drivers may still be stopped and cited for light transmission that violates the rules. Pennsylvania Vehicle Code §4524 addresses sun-screening materials and exceptions. A current inspection sticker is not proof that any darkness of window film is allowed.

Medical exemptions and factory glass

The Pennsylvania statute and PennDOT fact sheet describe special exceptions for certain factory installations and eligible medical circumstances. PennDOT explains a medical exemption process for colorless products that filter incoming ultraviolet light. An exemption is not a general authorization to choose any darker shade. The vehicle owner should contact PennDOT directly about eligibility, documentation and conditions before ordering a film that relies on an exemption.

Frequently asked questions

What tint is legal on passenger-car windows in Pennsylvania?
For passenger cars made in 1998 or later, PennDOT generally lists 70% minimum light transmission for windshield, front, rear side and rear windows. Exceptions and other vehicle classes differ.
Does my PA inspection sticker mean my tint is legal?
No. PennDOT says routine tint checks were removed from safety inspections, but unlawful tint may still result in a citation.
Do trucks and multipurpose vehicles have the same rear-window limits?
Not in PennDOT's standard chart. Front windows are treated differently from the rear on qualifying vehicle types. The exact classification matters.
Do you offer ceramic or specific film brands?
The shop's current products are not confirmed here. Call to ask which film options and specifications are available.
Is a medical exemption available for darker tint?
PennDOT has a medical exemption process, including criteria for colorless ultraviolet-filtering products. Contact PennDOT to confirm eligibility; an exemption is not automatic.
How do I know if new film will meet the rule?
The total light passing through glass and film must meet the limit for the actual window and vehicle type. Ask for the specifications and appropriate verification.
