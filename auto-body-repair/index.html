What an auto body inspection looks at

Vehicle bodies include exterior panels, doors, hoods, fenders, bumpers and the structural components underneath. Different cars use different construction. A visible scrape may be confined to a surface, while an impact that changes the fit of a panel can point to damage behind it. A proper discussion separates appearance from safety and function before deciding what repair to pursue.

Doors, hoods and liftgates need to close securely. Lamps and mirrors need to remain usable, and damaged edges should not create hazards. The condition and location of the damage matter more than the appearance alone. Photos can help describe a concern, but a picture may not show hidden mounting damage or the condition of components beneath a panel.

Common body concerns to discuss

  • A visible dent, crease or deformed exterior panel.
  • A door, hood or trunk that no longer lines up or latches correctly.
  • A damaged bumper cover or mounting area.
  • Cracked or loose exterior trim after an impact.
  • Unexpected gaps between panels or rubbing when a door opens.
  • Visible corrosion around a damaged panel or seam.
  • Broken lighting, glass or other nearby components.

These symptoms do not all require the same work. A loose exterior trim piece differs from a door that cannot latch; the second can affect vehicle safety. If the hood or door cannot stay secured, sharp metal protrudes, or damage affects handling, avoid driving until the condition is assessed. Do not force a bent panel back into place without knowing which components may be affected.

Panel dents, alignment and fit

A dent can be shallow or extend into a crease, edge or reinforced section. The location and depth of the damage affect the available repair approach. A panel that sits unevenly may reflect bent brackets or other hidden damage rather than a simple cosmetic issue. Scally's lists auto body repair, but a diagnosis and discussion are required before promising a particular panel repair method.

The appearance of a repaired panel may also depend on paint, finish and surrounding conditions. We do not advertise color-matching, paint booths, paintless dent removal or a fixed cosmetic outcome without approval from the shop. Ask what is included in the proposed work, how the panel will function afterward and if an outside specialist is needed.

After a small impact

An impact may affect a bumper cover, lighting, wheel alignment or the areas behind a panel. Even when the damage seems minor, note if the steering has changed, a light is loose or a door becomes difficult to close. Our auto light repair, wheel repair, alignment and glass repair pages cover related concerns.

The shop should not promise comprehensive crash repair or a specific frame procedure merely because auto body repair appears on the service list. Larger damage may call for a separate structural, mechanical or safety assessment. Explain what happened and ask for an evaluation of the affected systems. No fixed repair timeline, insurance handling or warranty is stated on this page.

Body corrosion and prevention

Scratches and damaged protective coatings can allow moisture to reach metal. Rust is different from a dent, and deeper corrosion can affect the strength of body parts. If damage shows bubbling paint, holes or an area that seems weak, point it out when discussing body work. Our rust repair and corrosion control page explains why surface treatment does not correct structural deterioration.

Auto body condition and PA inspections

PennDOT includes the vehicle body and chassis among the items checked during Pennsylvania safety inspections. Hazardous protruding metal, certain structural problems and other rejection criteria are evaluated under the state rules. Cosmetic marks alone are not a general guarantee of either passing or failing. See PennDOT consumer guidance and our PA inspection page.
