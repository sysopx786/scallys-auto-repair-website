What the check engine light means

Cars built for the U.S. since 1996 have an onboard diagnostic system, called OBD-II. It watches the engine, the emissions system and, on many cars, the transmission. When a reading falls outside its expected range, the computer stores a diagnostic trouble code and turns on the light.

The causes range from a loose gas cap to a failing sensor, a misfire or a catalytic converter problem. Two cars with the same code can need different repairs, which is why the code is only the first step.

Steady light or flashing light

  • Steady: the computer found a fault. Have it checked soon, but it usually isn't an emergency.
  • Flashing: usually a misfire bad enough to overheat and damage the catalytic converter. Reduce speed, avoid hard acceleration and get the car checked right away.
  • Light plus a temperature or oil pressure warning: pull over somewhere safe and shut the engine off.

How we diagnose it

Computer diagnostics is more than reading a code at a parts store. We look at the stored and pending codes, the freeze-frame data that shows what the engine was doing when the fault set, and live sensor readings. Then we test the circuit or part the data points to. That's how we tell a bad sensor from a wiring fault, or a vacuum leak from a fuel delivery problem.

Tell us what you noticed: when the light came on, whether the car runs differently, and whether it happened after filling up, in the rain or on a cold start. Those details shorten the search.

Computer diagnostics for other warning lights

The same approach applies to other warning lights and running problems: rough idle, hesitation, stalling or poor fuel economy with no light at all. Many problems leave clues in the computer before the light ever comes on. For electrical faults beyond the engine, see electrical repair.

Common causes we find

  • A loose, cracked or missing gas cap.
  • Oxygen sensors and other engine sensors. See emissions control.
  • Misfires from worn spark plugs or a failing ignition coil. See tune-ups and ignition.
  • Vacuum leaks and evaporative emissions leaks.
  • Fuel delivery problems. See fuel system service.
  • Catalytic converter efficiency faults.

The check engine light and your Pennsylvania inspection

Most cars registered in Delaware County need an emissions test, and on newer cars that test reads the OBD system. A check engine light that's on can keep the car from passing. Clearing the code right before the test doesn't help either: the system needs to finish its self-tests after a repair or a reset, which can take some normal driving. Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to drive with the check engine light on?
With a steady light and a car that runs normally, you can usually drive it to the shop. A flashing light, or a light with an overheating or oil pressure warning, means stop driving somewhere safe and call us at 610-626-4700.
Can a loose gas cap turn on the check engine light?
Yes. The evaporative emissions system can detect a leak at the cap. Tighten it until it clicks. If the light doesn't go off after several days of driving, have it checked.
Why not just read the code at a parts store?
A free code read tells you which system flagged a fault, not which part failed. Replacing parts based on the code alone often misses the real cause. Diagnosis tests the part or circuit before anything is replaced.
Will my car pass inspection with the check engine light on?
If your car needs the OBD emissions test, a lit check engine light can keep it from passing. We can diagnose and fix the fault, then test the car here.
The light went off by itself. Is the problem gone?
Not always. Some faults come and go, and the computer keeps the code in memory for a while after the light goes out. If the car runs differently, or the light keeps coming back, have it checked.
Do you diagnose imported cars?
Yes. We run computer diagnostics on domestic and imported vehicles. Call with the year, make and model.
