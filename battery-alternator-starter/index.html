How the battery, starter and alternator work together

The battery supplies the power to crank the engine through the starter motor. Once the engine runs, the alternator takes over: it powers the car's electrical systems and recharges the battery. Because each depends on the others, a fault in one can look like a fault in another. A weak alternator leaves the battery dead by morning; a failing starter can sound like a dead battery.

Signs of battery, charging or starting trouble

  • The engine cranks slowly, especially on cold mornings.
  • A single click, or rapid clicking, when you turn the key or press start.
  • The battery keeps going dead, or you need jump starts.
  • The battery or charging warning light comes on while driving.
  • Headlights dim at idle or flicker, or electronics act up.
  • A whining or grinding noise from the alternator or starter.
  • Corrosion on the battery terminals, or a swollen battery case.

A battery warning light while driving usually points to the charging system, not the battery. If it comes on, the car is running on battery power alone and may stall when it runs out. Head for the shop or somewhere safe rather than starting a long trip.

Battery testing and replacement

A battery can show normal voltage and still fail under load, which is why we test it the way the starter uses it. Heat and cold both shorten battery life, and a car that sits for weeks can drain a healthy battery. If it fails the test, we replace it with one that meets the size and rating your car calls for. Many newer cars with start-stop systems use special battery types that need the matching replacement.

Alternators and starters

We test the alternator's output with the engine running and the electrical load on. Low output, a whine or a charging light points to the alternator or its belt. For no-start problems, the sound matters: slow cranking points to the battery or connections, a single click with no crank often points to the starter or its circuit, and normal cranking with no start points elsewhere, such as fuel or ignition. See check engine light and diagnostics.

Jump starts

A jump start gets the car running; it doesn't tell you why the battery was dead. If it needs another jump within a day or two, or the battery light stays on, have the system tested. Follow the jump-start steps in your owner's manual, and don't jump a battery that's cracked, leaking or swollen.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I need a new battery?
Slow cranking, repeated jump starts and dim lights at startup are reasons to test it. A load test tells us whether the battery or the charging system is the problem.
Can a bad alternator look like a dead battery?
Yes. If the alternator isn't charging, the battery runs down and the car won't start. A new battery would go dead again, so we test the charging system too.
Why does my car click but not start?
Rapid clicking often means the battery is too weak to crank. A single click can point to the starter, its connections or a bad battery cable. We test before replacing anything.
Does a battery light mean I need a new battery?
Usually not. It means the car isn't charging the battery, which points to the alternator, its belt or wiring. Get it checked before the battery runs out.
Why does my battery die overnight?
A battery near the end of its life, an alternator that isn't fully charging, or something drawing power with the car off. We can test for that drain.
Can I keep driving after a jump start?
To get home or to the shop, usually yes. If the battery light stays on, the car may stall when the battery runs down, so avoid long trips and have it tested.
