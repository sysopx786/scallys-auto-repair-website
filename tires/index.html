Why tire care matters

Your tires are the only part of the car touching the road. Their tread, pressure and condition decide how well the car stops, steers and handles wet roads. Regular rotation and balancing spread the wear evenly, which helps the tires last longer and keeps the ride smooth.

Signs your tires need attention

  • The tire pressure warning light is on.
  • A tire keeps losing air, or you see a nail or screw in the tread.
  • Vibration in the steering wheel or seat at highway speed.
  • The tread is low or wearing unevenly.
  • A bulge, cut or crack in the sidewall.

A bulge in the sidewall means the tire's structure is damaged and it can fail suddenly. Don't drive on it farther than you have to; use the spare and bring it in. Uneven wear can come from low pressure, missed rotations, a bad alignment or worn suspension, so we look at the cause, not just the tire. See front end alignment and suspension repair.

Flat repair

A puncture in the main tread area can often be repaired from the inside of the tire. Damage in or near the sidewall, large cuts, or a tire that was driven flat usually can't be repaired safely, and the tire needs replacing. We take the tire off the wheel to check the inside before deciding.

Tire rotation

Front and rear tires wear at different rates, and front tires usually wear faster on front-wheel-drive cars. Rotation moves each tire to a new position so they wear evenly. Your owner's manual lists the interval and the rotation pattern for your car; many people have it done with every other oil change.

Wheel balancing

No tire and wheel is perfectly even in weight. Balancing spins the wheel on a machine and adds small weights to even it out. An out-of-balance wheel usually shows up as a vibration at highway speed, in the steering wheel if it's a front wheel. New tires get balanced when they're mounted, and a wheel that loses a weight needs rebalancing. A vibration that balancing doesn't fix can be a bent wheel, a damaged tire or a worn part, which we then check.

Tires and your Pennsylvania inspection

Tires are part of Pennsylvania's safety inspection. Tread worn below the state minimum, or damage like cuts and bulges, can keep a car from passing. As an official inspection station, we can check the tires, replace what's needed and finish the inspection here. See PA safety inspections.

Frequently asked questions

Can my flat tire be repaired?
Often, if the puncture is in the main tread and the tire wasn't driven flat. Sidewall damage and large cuts usually mean the tire needs replacing. We check the inside of the tire before deciding.
How often should tires be rotated?
Follow the interval in your owner's manual. Many drivers have it done with every other oil change.
Why does my car vibrate at highway speed?
An out-of-balance wheel is a common cause. A bent wheel, a damaged tire or worn suspension parts can cause similar vibration, so if balancing doesn't fix it, we look further.
What's the difference between balancing and alignment?
Balancing evens out the weight of each tire and wheel to stop vibration. Alignment adjusts the angles of the wheels so the car tracks straight and the tires wear evenly.
Will worn tires fail a Pennsylvania inspection?
They can. Tires are checked for tread depth and damage during the safety inspection. We can replace tires that fail and finish the inspection here.
Is it safe to drive on a tire with a bulge?
No. A bulge means the tire's internal structure is damaged and it can fail without warning. Put on the spare and have the tire replaced.
