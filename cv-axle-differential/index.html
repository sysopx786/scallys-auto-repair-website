How the drivetrain transfers power

The transmission sends engine power through the drivetrain to the driving wheels. A differential allows wheels on the same axle to turn at different speeds, which matters when cornering. CV joints can transmit power while steering or suspension changes the angle of a drive axle. Some vehicles have rear axle assemblies with bearings and seals that support axle shafts and retain lubricating fluid. The exact layout varies by front-, rear- or all-wheel-drive design.

Noise location and driving conditions help narrow a complaint, but sound travels through the body. A wheel bearing, tire, brake, axle or differential can sound similar from the driver's seat. A road-speed-related hum does not automatically call for differential work. A correct diagnosis considers which action produces the sound and the type of drivetrain fitted to the car.

Signs of possible axle or differential trouble

  • Clicking or popping on turns, especially while accelerating.
  • Vibration that becomes noticeable under power.
  • A steady hum, growl or whine from a wheel or axle area.
  • Visible damage or lost grease near a CV-joint boot.
  • Gear-oil stains near a differential housing or axle seal.
  • A clunk when accelerating, turning or changing gear.
  • A drivetrain warning or an unexpected change in vehicle handling.

These symptoms are not enough to name a failed part. A torn CV boot may expose a joint to contamination, but it does not prove replacement is the only option. A growl near a rear wheel might come from the tire or wheel hub rather than the axle bearing. If steering, vehicle stability or wheel support feels unsafe, do not continue driving without assessment.

Differential service and repair

Differentials contain gears and related bearings that need the proper lubricant. A differential may need fluid service at an interval specified by the vehicle maker, or repair if wear, damage or leakage is confirmed. A whining or rumbling noise can come from several sources, so a differential should not be replaced solely on the driver's impression of where the noise starts.

The proper gear oil specification depends on the vehicle and differential design. Some units have special requirements, so using an unspecified fluid is not a good substitute for the owner's manual. If you notice a leak, tell us when it began and where it appears. The source of the fluid needs confirmation before choosing a gasket, seal or another repair.

CV axles and joints

A CV axle uses joints that allow power transmission while the suspension moves and the steering turns. A worn joint may make a repeated clicking sound in a turn, but similar noises can come from other parts. A damaged protective boot can allow grease to escape and contamination to enter. Scally's lists CV axle and joint replacement; the correct scope depends on the joint, axle and vehicle.

Vibration during acceleration may be related to an axle, mount, tire or other rotating part. We recommend checking the condition rather than assuming a CV axle is responsible. Related services include motor and transmission mounts, wheel bearings and wheel alignment.

Rear axle bearings and seals

Some rear axle designs have bearings that support an axle shaft and seals that keep lubricant inside the assembly. Wear may create rear-end noise; a damaged seal may leave lubricant near a rear wheel. Not every rear-wheel noise or stain comes from an axle bearing or seal. An inspection helps distinguish these concerns from tire, brake and wheel hub problems.

Rear axle bearing and seal replacement are listed services, but the correct parts and procedure depend on the specific vehicle. Mention any rear-end vibration, fluid loss or recent impact when you call. An issue that affects wheel support or causes severe grinding needs prompt attention. Our wheel bearings and hubs page explains a related, but distinct, service.

Drivetrain maintenance and inspection

Follow the manufacturer's interval for differential lubricant and other drivetrain service. There is no one replacement mileage for every CV joint or axle bearing. Pay attention to new noises, torn boots or visible leaks, and have an issue checked before it becomes severe. Avoid driving if the vehicle has abnormal wheel movement, severe vibration or unstable handling. The transmission repair page covers other drivetrain systems.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my car click when turning?
A worn CV joint is one possibility, but other steering or suspension parts can make noises. Have the source confirmed.
Is a CV joint the same as a differential?
No. A CV joint transmits power through a changing angle. A differential distributes drive while allowing wheels to turn at different speeds.
Do you service differential fluid?
Yes. Differential fluid service is listed in Scally's catalog; the interval and specification depend on the vehicle.
Can a torn CV boot cause problems?
Yes. Grease can escape and contamination can enter, which may affect joint life. Inspect the condition before choosing repairs.
What causes an oil stain near a rear wheel?
An axle seal may be one possibility, but the fluid and source need to be identified first.
Do you replace rear axle bearings?
Yes. Rear axle bearing and seal replacement are listed services; the vehicle design determines what work applies.
