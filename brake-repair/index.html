How your brakes work

When you press the pedal, brake fluid carries that force through the brake lines to the calipers or wheel cylinders. They press the pads against the rotors, or the shoes against the drums, and the friction slows the car. Pads, shoes and rotors wear down with use. Brake fluid also absorbs moisture over time, which lowers its boiling point and can make the pedal feel soft after hard braking.

Signs you need brake repair

  • Squealing or squeaking when you brake.
  • Grinding, or a metal-on-metal sound.
  • The pedal pulses, or the steering wheel shakes, when you brake.
  • A soft, spongy or sinking pedal.
  • The car pulls to one side when you brake.
  • The brake or ABS warning light is on.
  • It takes longer to stop than it used to.

Each sign can have more than one cause, so we inspect before replacing parts. Grinding is often worn-through pads; a pulse is often the rotors; a soft pedal can be air, old fluid or a leak. If the pedal sinks toward the floor, or the brake warning light stays on with a soft pedal, it isn't safe to drive. Stop and call us, and we can talk about getting the car here.

Rotor resurfacing or replacement

Rotors wear thinner with every stop and can develop uneven spots that cause a pulsing pedal. Resurfacing machines a fresh, flat surface, as long as the rotor stays above the minimum thickness stamped on it. If it's below that, cracked or badly grooved, it gets replaced. We measure before deciding, so you only pay for what the rotor actually needs.

Brake fluid flush

A flush replaces old, moisture-laden brake fluid with fresh fluid of the type your manufacturer specifies, and bleeds any air out of the lines. Your owner's manual lists how often to do it. If a soft pedal comes from air in the system, bleeding the brakes is part of the fix.

Brakes and your Pennsylvania inspection

Brakes are part of Pennsylvania's safety inspection. Worn pads, damaged rotors or leaking lines can keep a car from passing. As an official inspection station, we can inspect the car, fix what failed and finish the inspection here. See PA safety inspections.

Frequently asked questions

Why do my brakes squeal?
Many pads have a built-in wear indicator that squeals when the pad gets thin. Squeals can also come from glazed pads, brake dust or loose hardware. Checking the pad thickness tells us which.
What does a grinding noise mean?
Usually the pad has worn down to its metal backing and is cutting into the rotor. Get it checked soon: it reduces braking and can turn a pad job into a rotor job.
Why does my steering wheel shake when I brake?
Uneven rotor thickness is a common cause. Worn suspension parts or wheel bearings can cause a similar shake, so we check those too.
Do you resurface rotors or replace them?
Both. We measure the rotor against its minimum thickness. If there's enough material and no cracks, we resurface it. If not, we replace it.
How often should brake fluid be changed?
Follow the interval in your owner's manual; it varies by manufacturer. We can also check the fluid when your car is in for other work.
Is it safe to drive with a soft brake pedal?
No. A soft or sinking pedal can mean a leak, air in the lines or a failing master cylinder. Stop driving the car and call us at 610-626-4700. We can talk about picking it up.
